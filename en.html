The prototype that came back to win

Fifty years after the 250 LM, Ferrari returned to the top class of endurance racing. The 499P answered with three consecutive 24 Hours of Le Mans victories and both 2025 world titles — writing the Prancing Horse back into the chronicle of the FIA WEC.

Fifty Years in the MakingTHE RETURN

CHAPTER 01 / 08

A wait spanning half a century ended at 4 p.m. on 11 June 2023.

499 + P

Ferrari prototypes have always carried the letter P — for Prototipo — preceded by the engine's per-cylinder displacement: 2,992 cc divided by six cylinders equals roughly 499. On 29 October 2022, at the Finali Mondiali in Imola, Ferrari unveiled the 499P: a four-wheel-drive hybrid prototype built for the top Le Mans Hypercar (LMH) class of the FIA WEC, operated by the works team together with AF Corse.

It was the marque's first factory assault on overall endurance honours since the 312 P of 1973 — half a century later. The project drew on the full depth of Ferrari's endurance legacy: more than twenty world titles since 1953, and nine overall wins at Le Mans before 1965.

Victory, written in the DNA

From 1949 to 1965 Ferrari collected nine overall wins at Le Mans; the era defined by the 166 MM, 250 Testa Rossa and 250 LM established endurance racing as the Prancing Horse's home ground. Every line of the 499P continues that lineage.

  • 1949–19659 overall Le Mans victories

  • TributeCar number 50, for the works prototype of 50 years prior

"The 499 P sees us return to compete for outright victory in the WEC series. We enter this challenge with humility, but conscious of a history that has taken us to over 20 world endurance titles and 9 overall victories at the 24 Hours of Le Mans."

— John Elkann, Ferrari Chairman · 2022 launch

Pole on debut

17 March 2023, the 1000 Miles of Sebring. Antonio Fuoco put the number 50 499P ahead of both defending-champion Toyotas in qualifying; in the race he held off the chasing Cadillac to finish third — a podium in the first top-class race in fifty years.

A body sculpted in the wind tunnel

The 499P's design was refined by the Ferrari Styling Centre under Flavio Manzoni, in lockstep with the sports-racing engineering department. Simple, sinuous shapes carry its full technical and aerodynamic content — an explicit expression of Ferrari's DNA.

Hybrid HeartPOWERTRAIN

CHAPTER 03 / 08

A mid-rear twin-turbo V6 and a front-axle motor — one hybrid system built for Le Mans.

F163CG · TWIN-TURBO V6

680 cv of combustion

A 3.0-litre 120° V6, twin-turbocharged and mid-mounted — architecture shared with the 296 family, rebuilt for endurance.

The hybrid powertrain pairs a mid-rear internal combustion engine with an electric motor on the front axle. The V6 biturbo F163CG (2,992 cc, 120°) shares its architecture with the 296 GT3 unit, thoroughly re-engineered by Ferrari; unlike the road car, it is a fully stressed member — the engine itself is part of the chassis.

The front-axle motor produces 200 kW, rule-limited to deployment above 190 km/h; energy flows from an 800 V battery developed with Formula 1 experience, fed by the ERS. Within the rules and BoP, the system delivers up to 500 kW to the wheels — the reserve behind every pass on the straights.

From road bloodline to stressed member

The engine shares its architecture with the 296 GT3, yet was re-engineered for endurance: as a fully stressed member it carries chassis loads directly, doing away with a sub-frame. The Xtrac seven-speed sequential gearbox in its magnesium casing, together with the F1-derived 800 V battery and ERS, completes a transmission and energy package built around 24 hours.

  • Architecture296 GT3-derived · fully stressed
  • DrivetrainXtrac 7-speed sequential · magnesium casing
  • ConsumablesTotalEnergies fuel · Shell Helix Ultra
ICE · V6 BITURBO

The V6's voice

The 120° vee lowers the centre of gravity and frees the exhaust; a dry sump copes with sustained lateral g — a V6 built to run for 24 hours.

ERS · ENERGY RECOVERY

Recover, release

Under braking the front motor charges the 800 V pack; on the straights the energy returns to the wheels — every lap at Le Mans is a complete energy cycle.

* Under Le Mans Hypercar rules, Balance of Performance adjusts each car's power ceiling dynamically; 500 kW is the regulatory system maximum, with per-round values set by the BoP. The 499P's theoretical combined output (500 kW ICE + 200 kW motor) exceeds the cap and is limited electronically on track.

SINGLE-SEATER LAYOUT

A cockpit of one

Near single-seater ergonomics, optimised for a three-crew endurance rotation.

The 499P's cabin follows single-seater philosophy: carbon race seat, racing wheel, functional switches lined along the central tunnel. Instrumentation centres on the driver's eye line; night lighting and button feel are designed for blind operation — at Le Mans, one second looking away is one second too long.

The same cockpit and ergonomics are handed unchanged to 499P Modificata owners (chapter 07): the client sits exactly where the race was won.

Fifty-three years, another world title

At the closing 8 Hours of Bahrain the three 499Ps held station to the flag: Manufacturers' and Drivers' world titles decided in a single night for Maranello — its first overall endurance crown since the 312 P of 1972, and the first Drivers' title ever awarded to a Ferrari crew in the top class.

Laurels on the bodywork

The world-champion laurel sits beside the shield and the Michelin man on the sidepod — the mark 499P now shares with the 312 P. On 18 December 2025, the crews drove both cars through the streets of Maranello to share the 53-year-overdue title with employees and fans.

PROJECT 255 · TRACK ONLY

870 cv of freedom

The client track car, unconstrained by FIA rules or BoP — unveiled at the 2023 Finali Mondiali to celebrate the Le Mans win.

The 499P Modificata was a first for Ferrari: a track-only car derived directly from a racer, yet free of any racing technical regulations. No BoP, no power cap, no 190 km/h threshold — the engineers returned everything the rulebook had constrained.

Cockpit and ergonomics are identical to the race winner: the owner sits exactly where Calado, Giovinazzi and Pier Guidi won Le Mans. From 2024, Modificata owners join the Sport Prototipi Clienti programme — Ferrari-run technical and logistical support, sharing the calendar with F1 Clienti and the XX Programme.

The winner's seating position

It shares the 499P's carbon monocoque, push-rod suspension and braking system; the V6 biturbo remains a fully stressed member. The bespoke work is about letting go: electronics, suspension and engine maps recalibrated for non-competitive driving, with start-assist and traction logic that let non-professional drivers approach the limit with confidence.

  • UnveiledOct 2023 · Finali Mondiali, Mugello
  • PositioningLimited · track-only · non-competitive
  • ProgrammeSport Prototipi Clienti (from 2024)

Push to Pass: seven extra seconds

A button on the back of the wheel releases an extra 120 kW (163 cv): available at full throttle, for no more than seven seconds per activation, with laps' worth of uses governed by battery balance and track layout — logic conceptually akin to F1's KERS. Nominal output is 520 kW (707 cv); activated, the total is 640 kW (870 cv).

  • Nominal520 kW (707 cv)
  • Activated640 kW (870 cv) · ≤7 s per use
  • Front axle200 kW motor · any speed

Two cars, opposite purposes

One built as the professional driver's instrument of ultimate performance; the other for the owner's pure driving pleasure: no stopwatch pressure, just fast-warming, consistent grip and limits that can be approached lap after lap. The official word for them: "two different worlds, conceived for completely different applications."
